# Carrier PCB

__One two-sided board on the PayloadSled's centre line__ (operator, 2026-09-11). The __tall side__ faces 270°, the camera port, and carries both XIAO stacks. The __low side__ faces 90°, the web, and carries the sensors. The board hangs off the web on two M3 standoffs, so it comes off the sled as one unit.

__This page is the carrier's one source of truth__ (operator, 2026-09-15). The layout drawing, the fit mock and the [nose-assembly drawing](https://github.com/jwilleke/js-rocket/blob/main/docs/designs/nose-assembly-6e81cfc.html) must all show what this page says. The drawing and the mock are generated from the same board, and the battery's position reaches both through `PCB-carrier-layout.json`. [PCB-carrier-design.md](PCB-carrier-design.md) is the design discussion: why each choice was made, and what is decided but not yet drawn. Where it gives a number, this page's number wins.

The sled side of the interface is [js-rocket#99](https://github.com/jwilleke/js-rocket/issues/99). This page is what the board is and what is still missing from it.

> __Corrected 2026-09-15 ([#33](https://github.com/jwilleke/js-rocket-avionics/issues/33)): the XIAO header rows are 15.24 mm apart, not 17.0.__ Seeed's footprint puts the pins at ±7.62 mm ([screenshot](../XIAO-ESP32S3-cam/PCB_Design_XIAO4.png)). The 17.0 was the centre of KiCad's SMD pads, and the fit mock printed with it could not take either XIAO. Both XIAOs and the L76K-GNSS now sit at ±7.62, and `verify_pins()` checks every XIAO-pattern pin against Seeed's footprint on every run. __The LSM6DSO32 is slid 3.45 mm toward +x__ (operator): its rows are narrower than the XIAO's, so they cannot both sit inside XIAO-ESP32S3-cam's. Its 9-pin row now runs outside the cam's row and its 5-pin row inside. A new check keeps every sensor joint clear of a XIAO header's plastic.

__The sensors run lengthwise, not turned.__ Turned 90°, a sensor's rows would cross the modules' rows. The BMP388 sits behind no module. The LSM6DSO32 sits behind XIAO-ESP32S3-cam, and its rows, 12.7 mm apart, cannot fit inside the cam's 15.24. So it is slid sideways until one row is outside: its holes sit 2.18 mm from the cam's, and its joints clear the cam's header plastic. The sensors stand __~1 mm proud of the board on their pins__, header plastic up against the sensor, so the plastic clears the tall side's solder joints on the low face.

__The battery sits behind the board__, between its low face and the web. Decided 2026-09-14; position B2 chosen 2026-09-15. The same day it was set at nose z 69.3–105.3, against the web, 1.2 mm aft of B2's first figure, so a cage's forward wall clears the BMP388. __This page is where the position is decided.__ `BATTERY_Y` in `gen_carrier.py` copies it for the drawing and the mock, and the sled's cage is built to it. What it moved, and what is still owed: [PCB-carrier-design.md](PCB-carrier-design.md#the-battery-sits-behind-the-carrier--decided-2026-09-14-layout-b2-2026-09-15).

## Three voltages, and only one of them is on this board

| Rail | Volts | Where it comes from | On the carrier? |
|---|---|---|---|
| __USB / VBUS__ | __5.0__ | A USB-C lead, only while one is plugged in | __No__ — it exists on each XIAO's `5V` pin (pin 14) and nowhere else. On battery power there is no voltage on it |
| __Battery__ | __3.7 nominal__ | The [LiPo](../LiPo-500mAh/LiPo-500mAh.md), to the JST, then by soldered pigtail to each XIAO's underside __BAT pads__ | As `VBAT`, between the JST and the wire pads. It does __not__ reach the headers |
| __Logic__ | __3.3__ | Each XIAO's own regulator | __Yes__ — see below |

__One regulator per load.__ XIAO-ESP32S3-cam's `3V3` feeds the +3V3 plane and the sensors. XIAO-ESP32S3-lora's `3V3` feeds only the L76K-GNSS, on its own net, `+3V3_LORA`, __kept off the plane__ — so no two regulators are ever paralleled. The carrier carries no regulator of its own.

```text
  LiPo 3.7 V
     |  lead from the forward end
     v
  JST-PH -- VBAT/GND --+-- pigtail --> XIAO-ESP32S3-cam  BAT pads
                       +-- pigtail --> XIAO-ESP32S3-lora BAT pads
  XIAO-ESP32S3-cam  3V3 --> +3V3 plane --> LSM6DSO32, BMP388 (BMP388 CS tied high: I2C, #19)
  XIAO-ESP32S3-lora 3V3 --> +3V3_LORA  --> L76K-GNSS
```

__JST pin 1 is `VBAT` in the generator, and it must be checked against the battery's red lead before soldering.__ JST-PH polarity is not standardised, and reversing a LiPo into a XIAO destroys it. The silkscreen marks `+` at the JST and at each `VBAT` wire pad.

__Everything else is powered by the XIAO it plugs onto__, through that module's B2B connector: the [Wio-SX1262](../Wio-SX1262-LoRa/Wio-SX1262-LoRa.md) and the [Sense-camera-board](../Sense-camera-board/Sense-camera-board.md) with its camera and [microSD](../microSD/microSD.md). The [PS1240 buzzer](../PS1240-buzzer/PS1240-buzzer.md) is not on the board: it sits against the nose wall on two leads from the `BUZZER` and GND wire pads. The antennas are on the sled ([Antennas.md](../Antennas/Antennas.md)), so __no RF crosses the carrier__.

### Charge through the Sense stack, and only that one

__Operator, 2026-09-08.__ For charging alone it makes no difference — both XIAOs have a charger and both sit on the same battery. __The decision is about which port is permanently committed__, because [#1](https://github.com/jwilleke/js-rocket-avionics/issues/1)'s service pigtail occupies whichever one it is wired to:

- __[XIAO-ESP32S3-cam](../XIAO-ESP32S3-cam/XIAO-ESP32S3-cam.md), the Sense stack__ — the only one that reaches the microSD, and the one being reflashed constantly, since its firmware does not exist yet
- __[XIAO-ESP32S3-lora](../XIAO-ESP32S3-lora/XIAO-ESP32S3-lora.md)__ — runs __stock Meshtastic and nothing else__ — [the firmware rule](../XIAO-ESP32S3-lora/XIAO-ESP32S3-lora.md#firmware--stock-meshtastic-only-updated-on-purpose-frozen-for-flight). That is the whole reason there are two modules. A service port on it invites exactly what the two-module split exists to prevent

__So XIAO-ESP32S3-lora's USB-C is not used in the flight build.__ __And still one at a time__ — [Two chargers on one battery](../LiPo-500mAh/LiPo-500mAh.md#two-chargers-on-one-battery) is why.

## Build rules

- __4-layer, solid ground plane.__ A few dollars more at this size; fixes return paths and coupling from the camera's DVP flex
- __Module footprints, not bare chips.__ A bare LSM6DSO32 is an LGA-14 at 2.5 × 3 mm and is not hand-solderable. Soldering breakouts down still gives one rigid assembly — apart from the battery pigtails, which are unavoidable
- __Solder the headers — no loose sockets.__ Conformal coat after bench testing
- __Assembly order: the low side first.__ Solder the sensors, standing ~1 mm proud, and trim their tails on the tall face, then the tall side's modules. A sensor joint on the tall face sits right beside a module header's plastic
- __The screws stop inside the standoffs.__ Each standoff has a 4.4 mm hole through the board, so neither sits under a module. __M3 × 10__ through the 3 mm web leaves 3 mm short of the board; a longer one pokes into the USB-C plug room
- __The battery pigtails are anchored to the board, not held by their solder joints.__ Wire spec and anchor are in [LiPo-500mAh.md](../LiPo-500mAh/LiPo-500mAh.md#the-pigtail-is-the-fragile-part-and-the-solder-joint-must-not-be-the-anchor), which owns them
- __Mark polarity__ at the JST and the `VBAT` pads

## Decided, not yet on the board

__The generator draws none of these yet__, so the layout drawing, the fit mock and the tables above do not show them either. Each one is decided, and each one adds parts or changes a net before copper is ordered ([#11](https://github.com/jwilleke/js-rocket-avionics/issues/11)). The reasoning is in [PCB-carrier-design.md](PCB-carrier-design.md).

- __Q1, the charge-isolation FET__ — plugging USB into XIAO-ESP32S3-cam takes XIAO-ESP32S3-lora off the battery, and the Wio-SX1262 and L76K-GNSS with it. __No beacon while USB is in.__ AO3401A, gate on XIAO-ESP32S3-cam's `5V` pin, which carries nothing on battery power, so it cannot switch off in flight ([why](PCB-carrier-design.md#q1--the-charge-isolation-fet), [#30](https://github.com/jwilleke/js-rocket-avionics/issues/30))
- __U1, a faster charger__ — MCP73831, 370 mA, fed from the same `5V` pin. __Q2__, which takes XIAO-ESP32S3-cam's own charger off the battery while USB is in, is recommended and waiting on the operator's OK ([#30](https://github.com/jwilleke/js-rocket-avionics/issues/30))
- __Two data links__, each behind an open 2-pin jumper ([why](PCB-carrier-design.md#two-data-links-each-behind-an-open-jumper--decided-not-yet-in-the-generator), [#26](https://github.com/jwilleke/js-rocket-avionics/issues/26))
- __The PS1240 driven from two pins__ in opposite phase, for about +6 dB (operator, 2026-09-15, [#25](https://github.com/jwilleke/js-rocket-avionics/issues/25)). `TP_BUZ_G` becomes the second drive net instead of `GND`, and both holes stay where they are. Which XIAO-ESP32S3-cam pin is still open

## Where it actually is

| Stage | State |
|---|---|
| 2a — outline, standoffs, stackup | __done__ |
| 2b — nets and footprints | __done__ — every footprint placed and netted |
| 2c — placement | __done in the generator__, pending the operator's review of the drawing |
| 2d — routing | __not started__. DRC reports __0 violations__ and 10 unconnected items, which are exactly the unrouted signal nets. Autorouting is wrong here — [#15](https://github.com/jwilleke/js-rocket-avionics/issues/15) |
| 2e — gerber + drill export | chain proven; needs a finished board |

__The one DRC exception__ is in `PCB-carrier.kicad_dru`, which the generator writes: Würth's standoff land pattern puts its pads right at the edge of its own hole, which is the maker's design. It is allowed for the two standoffs and nowhere else.

__Checks owed before fabrication__ — [PCB-carrier-design.md](PCB-carrier-design.md#checks-owed-before-building) keeps the list: the standoff part in stock and JST pin 1 against the battery. Meshtastic's GPS pins are settled — D6/D7, read off the board (#6).

> __`fab/gerbers/` holds a complete-looking set from an old board.__ It is the single most likely way a wrong board gets ordered — it is complete, it is in the repo, and it looks done. __Regenerate, never reuse__ — [#16](https://github.com/jwilleke/js-rocket-avionics/issues/16).

## Read the board, not the script

An earlier generator revision put GPS on pins 6/7 and I2C on 4/5 — __D5/D6 and D3/D4, every one off by one__. It was caught by __reading the mapping back out of the saved board__, not by trusting the script's output. The generator now names pins (`XIAO_PIN["D6"]`) so the numbers never appear by hand.

> __It happened again, twice.__ [#18](https://github.com/jwilleke/js-rocket-avionics/issues/18): the whole `+8.5 mm` row mirrored end for end, `+3V3` and `GND` on __D9 and D8__. [#21](https://github.com/jwilleke/js-rocket-avionics/issues/21): a back-mounted XIAO netted as if on the front, `BUZZER` on the __5V pin__.
>
> __Now the checks run, from two sources each.__ `verify_pins()` holds each XIAO's footprint table against the underside photograph, the L76K-GNSS's turned Seeed drawing against the same drawing turned by eye, and each sensor — on the back, where KiCad's top view mirrors it — against its front photograph carried there by two independent routes. `verify_clearances()` checks every through-hole against every other across both sides, and against the standoffs and the BMP388's legs. Putting past bugs back fails the build: a mirrored L76K-GNSS, a sensor placed without the back-side mirror, and a sensor pin moved onto a module pin are all caught.

## The KiCad sources are in this folder

`PCB-carrier.kicad_pcb`, `PCB-carrier.kicad_pro` and `PCB-carrier.kicad_dru` sit beside this page, with `PCB-carrier-layout.json`, which the diagram reads. ## Fit mock — print it before ordering copper

__[`fit-mock/PCB-carrier-fit-mock.stl`](fit-mock/PCB-carrier-fit-mock.stl)__ — the generated board's own body, exported by KiCad, so it cannot drift from the design. Regenerate it after any board change with [`gen_carrier_fitmock.py`](../scripts/gen_carrier_fitmock.py); do not edit it. It is a copy with three changes, never the board:

- __Every hole opened 0.3 mm__ on diameter, because printed holes come out small: header holes Ø1.3 (Ø1.0 on the real board), standoff holes Ø4.7 (4.4)
- __An arrow cut through the board, pointing forward__ — toward the nose tip — at the forward end, beside the forward standoff. It shows which end is forward. Printed low side down, the top face is the tall side
- __Four small marker holes at the edges__ where the battery's two ends fall, nose z 69.3 and 105.3, read from `PCB-carrier-layout.json`. The battery is not a footprint, so nothing else on the board shows it

__1.6 mm thick__, like the real board — KiCad exports the core alone at 1.51 mm, so the script scales it — which puts the header plastic at its true height. Two mocks printed on 2026-09-15 are scrap: the first at 1.0 mm, and both with the XIAO rows 17.0 mm apart (#33).

__No labels on it__ (operator, 2026-09-15). Raised and engraved silkscreen was tried, and the slicer dropped it: strokes of ~0.15 mm against a 0.42 mm line, 21 mm of extrusion on each label layer. __What goes where is on paper instead__ — [`PCB-carrier-template.pdf`](PCB-carrier-template.pdf), both sides at full size, generated by [`gen_carrier_template.py`](../scripts/gen_carrier_template.py) from the layout and the mock's own holes. Print it at 100 % and check its 100 mm bar with a ruler.

__Print:__ PETG HF, flat, __low side down__ (the STL has it at z 0), 0.2 mm layers, 2 walls, no support, no brim. About 5 g and 23 minutes. __Before sending a plate, prove it in the G-code__: every hole open on every layer, and the height 1.6 mm. A mesh swapped into an old plate keeps the old plate's position, and the 2026-09-15 swap left the mock floating 0.2 mm above the bed, 1.8 mm thick, until the G-code showed it.

__What it checks, with the bench parts:__

1. __Both XIAO stacks and the L76K-GNSS push onto their holes__ without forcing — the header row spacing and every pin position, before copper
2. __The sensors on the low side__, if their headers are fitted, land in their own holes, clear of the tall side's pins
3. __The whole populated mock passes a 40 mm bore__ — the stacks' corners are worked at r 14.78 and 15.67 against the door's r 20
4. __The battery between its marks__, held 5.25 mm off the low side as the cage holds it: its lead reaches the JST around the edge
5. __Screw it through the standoff holes__ — to spacers or the sled, once #99 is cut — and see how the span behaves. PETG is roughly ten times more flexible than FR4, so a mock that holds its shape says the real board will; a mock that sags says nothing either way

If the pins will not pass, open the holes with a 1.2–1.3 mm drill, or regenerate with a larger `HOLE_GROW`.

## Ordering

__Do not order copper before breadboarding.__ A layout error costs ~$43 and __two weeks__; a wiring error costs minutes. The gate is [#4](https://github.com/jwilleke/js-rocket-avionics/issues/4); the ordering epic is [#11](https://github.com/jwilleke/js-rocket-avionics/issues/11). Three copies from OSH Park: one to fly, two spares.

__The two standoffs are the sled interface.__ [js-rocket#99](https://github.com/jwilleke/js-rocket/issues/99) drills the web at their positions, so moving either changes the sled.
